About

I'm a PhD student in ECE at Purdue. My work covers satellite image change detection, multivariate time-series forecasting on sensor data, and model compression for edge deployment.

The thread connecting these is practicality. Foundation models are computationally expensive, largely opaque, and rarely deployable outside data-center settings. I think efficiency and interpretability are prerequisites for a model that can actually be used, not secondary concerns.

That shapes two directions in my research: making models leaner without sacrificing what they have learned, and building representations that capture structured temporal and spatial dependencies rather than shallow statistical patterns.

Problems I’m Interested In

My research is motivated by one constraint: models need to work in practice, not just on benchmarks. That means being efficient enough to deploy on constrained hardware, interpretable enough to diagnose failure, and structured enough to generalize. Accuracy alone is not sufficient.

Efficient learning and model compression. Overparameterization is the norm in modern deep networks. I am interested in identifying which structural components are actually necessary, how redundancy can be eliminated without performance loss, and whether inductive biases can substitute for learned parameters. I treat compression as an architectural question, not a post-hoc engineering step.

Temporal and spatial reasoning with semantics. Sensor networks, satellite imagery streams, and other real-world systems exhibit structured dynamics over time and space. Most sequence models reduce this to short-range correlation. I am interested in representations that capture long-range dependencies and spatial semantics, including the ability to disentangle meaningful change from nuisance factors like illumination shift or sensor drift.

Graph-based learning. Graphs provide a principled inductive bias for relational and structured data. I apply GNNs to model cross-channel dependencies in multivariate time series and relational structure in visual scenes, with applications in anomaly detection, forecasting, and change detection.

Diffusion models and structured inference. Diffusion models interest me beyond synthesis tasks. Their connection to score-based and energy-based methods makes them a natural framework for structured inference and inverse problems, particularly in settings where domain constraints can be incorporated directly into the generative process.
